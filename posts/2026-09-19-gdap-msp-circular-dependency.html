GDAP for MSPs: Fixing the Circular Dependency Nobody Warns You About

September 2026 • By Philipp Schmidt
MSPEntra IDGDAPSecurityBest Practices

Granular Delegated Admin Privileges replaced the old delegated admin model with something more precise and, in the process, introduced a failure mode the old model never had: it is possible to lose the access you need to fix your own GDAP relationship, because fixing it requires exactly the access that is gone.

The blunt version: you need to be signed in to the customer tenant as Global Administrator to grant yourself GDAP access — which only works if you already have a way into that tenant that is not GDAP itself.

In short

If GDAP is your only path into a customer tenant and the relationship lapses, there is no independent way back in. The fix is not a GDAP setting — it is not depending on GDAP alone: a documented break-glass path per customer, monitored renewal status rather than assumed auto-renewal, and a least-privilege role set mapped to security groups rather than assigned to individuals.

Where the circular dependency comes from

GDAP relationships are established through Partner Center, require the customer tenant to accept the request, and are then mapped from MSP security groups to specific roles. If that relationship lapses — expiry, accidental removal, a renewal failure — restoring it through Partner Center is the normal path. But if the MSP's only standing access to the customer tenant was the GDAP relationship, and the relationship is what broke, there is no independent way back in without either the customer's own admin acting, or a break-glass credential maintained outside GDAP entirely.

The practical fix is not a GDAP setting. It is not depending on GDAP as your only path in. Maintain a documented, secured break-glass account in each customer tenant, or a customer-controlled process for granting emergency access, specifically for the case where GDAP itself is broken. That is operational overhead most MSPs would rather not carry — the alternative is being locked out with no route back except asking a customer to re-establish a relationship they may not know how to create.

Auto-renewal is not reliable enough to trust unmonitored

GDAP relationships carry renewal requirements that have shifted with Microsoft's partner security changes, and MSPs report renewals that should have gone through automatically failing without a notification reaching anyone who would act. Treat expiration as a monitored event, not an assumed non-issue: check Partner Center relationship status on a schedule rather than waiting for a customer to notice their MSP lost access. A relationship a week from expiring should trigger action well before it lapses, because re-establishing it afterwards involves the customer again — slower, and dependent on their availability.

What roles an MSP actually needs

There is no Microsoft-published "here are the GDAP roles a typical MSP needs," so every MSP solves the same least-privilege problem independently. A reasonable starting set, scoped by function rather than granting broad admin by default:

  • Helpdesk: User Administrator, Authentication Administrator — password and MFA method resets, basic user support
  • M365 administration: Exchange, SharePoint and Teams Administrator — day-to-day service configuration
  • Security: Security Administrator and Security Reader, split between who needs to act and who only needs to observe
  • Full administration: Global Administrator, reserved for a small number of senior technicians and scoped through PIM activation rather than standing assignment

Mapping each function to its own security group, rather than assigning roles to individuals, is what makes this maintainable. Role changes become group membership changes rather than a renegotiation of the relationship itself.

Managing this at scale

Manually managing role-to-group mappings across dozens or hundreds of customer tenants does not hold up past a handful. Tooling that treats the mapping as a template and reapplies it across managed tenants is a meaningfully different operational model from configuring each tenant individually and hoping they stay consistent. Layering PIM on top — technicians eligible for a role rather than standing in it — adds real security value, at the cost of stacking PIM's own activation-verification gap onto GDAP's reliability issues. Worth doing deliberately, not assumed to work end to end because each piece is documented separately.

Bottom line

GDAP is the correct model: precise, auditable access instead of blanket delegated admin rights. It still fails in ways that are hard to recover from when the only path into a tenant runs entirely through GDAP itself. A break-glass path outside it, monitored renewal, and a documented role set mapped to security groups turn GDAP from a fragile dependency into what it is supposed to be.

Glossary

GDAP
Granular Delegated Admin Privileges — the Microsoft partner model granting scoped, time-bound administrative access to a customer tenant, replacing blanket delegated admin rights.
Partner Center
Where GDAP relationships are requested, accepted, monitored and renewed. The relationship status here is what needs scheduled checking.
Break-glass account
An emergency-access account maintained outside the normal access path — here, outside GDAP — so a failure of that path does not lock you out entirely.
Role-to-group mapping
Assigning GDAP roles to MSP security groups rather than individuals, so staffing changes become group membership changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the need Global Admin to grant GDAP access circular dependency actually happen?

Yes. If an MSP's only access into a customer tenant is the GDAP relationship itself and that relationship lapses, restoring it may require access the MSP no longer has. A documented break-glass path outside GDAP, maintained per customer tenant, is the practical mitigation.

How do I fix GDAP auto-renewal failures?

Monitor Partner Center relationship status on a schedule rather than assuming renewal happens silently and correctly. Act on relationships approaching expiry well before the deadline, since re-establishing a lapsed relationship requires customer involvement and is slower.

What GDAP roles does an MSP actually need?

There is no Microsoft-published template. A reasonable starting point splits roles by function — helpdesk, M365 administration, security, full administration — each mapped to its own security group, with Global Administrator reserved for a few technicians and scoped through PIM activation rather than standing assignment.

How do GDAP, PIM and multi-tenant tooling fit together?

Management tooling handles role-to-group mappings as templates across tenants, solving the scale problem. Layering PIM makes technician access time-bound rather than standing, at the cost of inheriting PIM's own activation-verification gaps alongside GDAP's reliability issues.

How many GDAP relationships should an MSP have per customer tenant?

One per customer tenant is standard, with access differentiated through multiple role-mapped security groups inside that relationship rather than multiple separate relationships.
